Cinematic · look and mood

Color the world, not the UI

Cinematic grades the game world like a film: a tint that follows the time of day and the zone you're in, soft dark edges, a warm glow in inns, weather that grays the scene, and letterbox bars. Your UI is never tinted.

Where it lives

Two pages under Atmosphere

Open Cinematic's settings and pick Atmosphere. Its General page has the letterbox, vignette, inn glow, weather and depth of field, and a grid of where each one shows. The Tint page has the color grading: a preset, the time of day and each zone's mood. (Its third page, Graphics+, is the game's own graphics settings, with a guide of its own, and its fourth, Audio, is the music and sound.)

Both pages share one Enable Visual Effects switch. Turning it off removes every effect at once: letterbox, tint, time of day, zone tints, vignette, inn glow, weather and depth of field. Each keeps its own settings for when you turn it back on.

The effects show in CineMode. If you turn off Start in CineMode on the main page, they lie over your normal UI instead. That's how the Subtle profile works: the look, with nothing faded.

Try it

Pick a preset and drag through the day. The colors are the addon's own, laid over a painted scene. The game blends them over the real world, so in the game it looks softer.

The Tint page

Presets

The preset is the color laid over the world. Pick one on the Tint page, then set its Strength. Every preset works with the vignette, weather and inn glow on top.

PresetWhat it does
NoneNo color tint.
Warm / golden hourSoft orange, like late-afternoon light.
CoolPale blue, crisp and wintry.
NightDeep blue and darker, like moonlight.
Dusk gradientOrange at the top fading to purple at the bottom, like a sunset.
Sepia-ishWarm brownish yellow, old-photo warmth. The world can't be truly desaturated, hence the "ish".
DreamyA faint warm glow added over the scene. Soft and hazy.
CustomYour own color, chosen with the Custom color swatch.
Time of dayChanges through the day: blue at night, pink at dawn, clear in the day, golden in the evening and purple at dusk.
ZoneA mood for the zone you're in. Zones without one are left untinted.
Zone + time of dayBoth together: a desert at night is warm sand darkened by moonlight. Cinematic starts with this one.

Subtle drift (on to start with) lets the strength wander up and down by a few percent over a few minutes, so a still scene feels a little more alive. Drift amount sets how far. The drift pauses while the Tint page is open, so you see the strength you set.

Time of day

From moonlight to golden hour

The Time of day and Zone + time of day presets follow a clock through five phases, blending smoothly between them. Under Follows, pick the game's time (matching the game's day and night) or your computer's clock.

0006121824
  • Nighttill about 5:00, from 21:30
  • Dawnpink, about 6:30
  • Dayclear, 8:00 to 17:00
  • Golden hourabout 18:30
  • Duskpurple, about 20:00

Time of day strength sets how strongly the clock's color applies. Lower it for softer nights; at 0 only the zone mood is left. Under Phases, click a phase's color to choose your own, give it a strength of its own (Night starts at 65%), or press its Reset to go back to the built-in one.

To see the tint at another hour without waiting, pick one in Preview time beside the preset. It's only a preview: the real clock comes back when you leave the page.

Zone moods

Every zone its own color

With a Zone preset, each zone has a mood of its own: pale blue snowfields, sandy deserts, sickly plaguelands, a smoky Ironforge, a golden Westfall and many more. On retail the list covers every expansion, from Outland's fel-scorched Hellfire to Midnight's Voidstorm. Zones the addon doesn't know are left untinted.

    At a zone border the old mood fades out, the scene stays untinted for a moment, then the new mood fades in, so snow never turns red under a volcano's ember tint. The pause is longer in the air, where you can still see back over the old zone.

    Setting your own

    1. Stand in the zone, or in the town or port within it, and open the Tint page's Zone tints section. It shows the mood here, and whether it's built in or your choice.
    2. Under Change, pick the zone or this area. An area's choices win over its zone's.
    3. Pick a Preset here to replace your main preset in that place (Night in Duskwood, say, or None in a city), or pick a Custom color to replace its built-in mood in the Zone presets.
    4. Your zone tints lists every place you've changed, each with a Reset.

    Some towns already have a softer mood than their zone, such as Theramore Isle, Booty Bay, Ratchet and Gadgetzan.

    The General page

    Letterbox, vignette, inns and weather

    Letterbox

    Black bars slide in at the top and bottom of the screen. Set their Bar height (up to a quarter of the screen each) and Bar opacity.

    Vignette

    Soft dark edges that draw the eye to the middle. Works with any tint. Set it with Vignette strength.

    Inn glow

    With Inn glow amount above Off, stepping into an inn partly lifts the outdoor tint (a gloomy marsh, a moonlit night) and fades in a soft firelit glow, colored by the zone: cooler in the snow, greener in a swamp. It blends over a few seconds at the door. The further right, the brighter.

    Weather

    Rain grays and cools the scene, snow turns it pale blue and a sandstorm gives it a dusty orange haze, more strongly the heavier the weather. It lifts under a roof and blends in as a storm builds. Works with any tint, None included.

    Weather needs a game version that reports the weather to addons. Where it doesn't, the weather row does nothing.

    Where each one shows

    The Show in grid at the top of the General page has a row each for the letterbox, tint, vignette and weather, laid out like every other grid in Cinematic. Always shows it all through CineMode. The Combat dropdown picks In combat, Calm Timer (after a fight, until it runs out), Out of combat, or In a camera mode (flights, AFK, cozy and the rest). Location picks places. With nothing ticked, the effect is off. That's how "keep the letterbox up through fights" or "tint only while a camera runs" is set.

    In passing

    Depth of field

    With Haze the screen edges in the camera modes ticked, a soft haze lies around the screen's edges while a camera runs, as if the camera had focused on you. Each camera sets its own strength on its Camera Settings page. The cameras guide covers it.

    Commands

    See it now

    Type these in the chat box.

    /cine tinttestGoes through each time of day in turn, its tint and its title, with the AFK camera. Type it again to stop.
    /cine doftestShows the depth-of-field haze for 30 seconds. Again to stop.
    Questions

    Good to know

    Why isn't my action bar tinted?
    On purpose. The tint lies over the world only, so health bars and text keep their true colors.
    Nights are too dark for me.
    Lower Time of day strength, or give Night a lower strength of its own under Phases. At 0 you keep just the zone moods.
    The tint is right but the hour is wrong.
    Check Follows: game time follows the game's day and night, which may not match your own clock. Switch to your computer's clock if you'd rather.
    Can I take a screenshot with the look but no UI?
    Yes. Set a key for Hide the UI on the Keybinds page. It works like the game's Alt+Z, but the tint, letterbox and time-of-day title stay on screen. Press it again (or Alt+Z) to bring the UI back.
