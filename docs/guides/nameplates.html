Cinematic · nameplates and names

Only the plates that matter

In CineMode, nameplates and names over heads fade like the rest of the UI. You choose, for each kind of unit and each place, which stay: enemies in a fight, your group in a battleground, nothing at all in a city. Outside CineMode they're left to the game.

The Nameplates page

Grids of kinds and places

The Nameplates page, in the UI Manager, says which nameplates and names stay up in CineMode. Each grid has a row per kind of unit and these columns:

  • AlwaysShown everywhere, in fights and out.
  • In CombatIn fights and for the Calm Timer after. In PvP Combat instead: only while you're PvP flagged.
  • Enemy targetedOut of fights, while you have a living enemy targeted.
  • Friend targetedOut of fights, while you have anything else targeted: friends, NPCs, dead enemies.
  • PlacesPvP (flagged, outside battlegrounds and arenas), BGs & Arenas, Open World (inns included), Cities, Grouped (in a party or raid) and Instances (dungeons, scenarios and raids).

Ticking a box also turns those nameplates on in the game's own options, so they can show at all. Like the frame grids, any row can be turned into a rule of its own, for AND, NOT and more places.

The page's Enable page switch turned off leaves nameplates and names alone, and the game's own options decide, in CineMode and out.

    Friendly Plates in Dungeons and Raids

    In dungeons and raids the game won't let addons fade friendly nameplates, so each kind (players, pets and minions, NPCs) is either all on or all off there: Hidden or Shown, and for players and NPCs also With a friendly target (on while you have one of that kind targeted, out of fights; every plate of that kind shows then, not just your target's). Friendly players' setting covers their pets too, so with pets shown, friendly players' plates show as well. The game won't switch nameplates on or off during a fight: they stay as the fight found them.

    To start with, friendly players and NPCs show with one targeted, and pets never.

    Names on faded plates

    A name, with the plate hidden

    In CineMode, away from dungeons and raids, nameplates stay on but faded, and the game takes its own names away from units that have a plate. So where a name is kept up, Cinematic draws it on the faded plate instead, made to look like the game's names over heads: red for hostile units, yellow for neutral ones, green for friendly NPCs and blue for friendly players (green while they're PvP flagged), with no outline, and smaller further off.

    Beyond nameplate range the game's own names take over, so a name can shift a little in size or place as its unit crosses that range.

    Change their look on the Names page in the Style hub, with a preview of a hostile, neutral and friendly name and your own at the top: whether to style them at all (unticked, they look as the plates draw them), three presets to start from (Like the game's names, Bold and Subtle), whether to color them by how the unit sees you and how to color friendly players (blue, class colors or white), their outline and opacity, and their size and height. On retail they keep one size at any distance unless you untick it; on WoW Forever you set their size and height for a unit close to the camera and one far off, and names in between follow perspective.

    Style

    The Target Indicator

    With no target frame on screen, it can be hard to tell what you've targeted. The Target Indicator puts a mark on your target's nameplate, shown even while the rest of the plate is faded or shows just its name, and marks of their own on your focus and on the unit under the mouse. Tick its row in the Cinematic Plate Parts grid to say when; set their looks on the Nameplates page, first in the Style hub. Its Enable page switch turns all the marks and their colors off at once, keeping the settings. The Cinema profile turns it on all through CineMode.

    The page is a grid with a row for each kind, to start with: Aggro on you, any enemy attacking you; Target; Focus; Mouseover, which works when you hover the unit itself, not just its plate, and takes only the marks over the plate (arrow, chevron or diamond: the others would hang in the air where its plate is hidden); and Default, always last, for every plate no other row fits: the game's own look to start with, no mark and the game's colors, until you give it a mark or a plate color. A plate takes its whole look (mark, size, opacity) from the first row that fits it, top to bottom, and the rows below aren't looked at: so with Target on top, your target that's also your focus looks as the Target row says, and a row with its mark on - really means none. Only its plate color can come from further down: a row whose plate color is - leaves that to the next row that fits. At the top of the page, one large preview plate shows the look of the row you last clicked or added (Default to start with): its mark, its name and health bar colors, your target's border, at its opacity and size, with debuffs, a buff and a cast bar drawn in so it looks like a real plate. Each row is one line: arrows to move it up or down, its Show tick, a small preview of its mark on a plate (at its opacity), its name, and chips for whatever's set apart from the usual: an opacity in amber, a When other than Always in blue (Never when nothing's ticked), roles, the parts its plate color colors, a size. Hover a row for all of it. Click a row (or its +) to open its drawer underneath: Name, a name of your own for the row (empty: its kind's); Row, what it's for (not on Default's own); Mark (style, color, Moving, and at the end its size and opacity); Plate (color and the parts it colors, and at the end its size and opacity); and When. The size and opacity boxes change with the mouse wheel, 5% a step, or type a number and press Enter. Searching the page for a setting opens the drawer it's in. Aggro on you fits any enemy attacking you; on your target it needs to be above Target to change anything. Aggro on you's cells start on Your target's, taking the Target row's, except its color, which is red: so a target that attacks you keeps its look and only its mark turns red. Change any of them to set it apart. It has its own size and Moving tick.

    Add row, under the grid, puts a new row at the end with its drawer open, for Any unit to start with. Its Row line says what a row is for, and changing it changes the row in place, its settings with it: a Kind, Target, Focus, Mouseover, Aggro, Units or Default. Aggro and Units open a second dropdown of ticks for which of them. Aggro: who the enemy's attacking, You or Not you (someone friendly other than you: a group member, a pet or another player), and their role, Tank, Healer or DPS (no role set counts as DPS; a pet only with all three ticked), at least one of each. Not you and Healer, say, is any enemy on another healer; You and Not you with Healer and DPS is any enemy on a non-tank. Units, any mix of Normal NPC and Elite NPC (living NPCs by rank: elite, rare elite and bosses are elite), Players and Minions (pets, totems and guardians): both NPCs is any living NPC, all four any unit, and one row can be for, say, elite NPCs and players. Then Who, ticks too: Enemy, Neutral and Friendly, all of them for any (Neutral only with NPCs ticked: players and minions are friendly or enemy; Aggro has no Who). Then Distance: Any distance, or just units within or past about 11, 28 or 40 yards of you. 11 and 28 are the game's interact distances; 40 asks a 40-yard spell on your action bars (harmful for enemies, helpful for friends), so with none there a 40-yard row fits no unit past 28 yards. To fade plates far off, add Any unit past 40 yards with a low Opacity and move it under Target, so your target stays clear. A kind, Who and Distance already on the grid makes another row of it, numbered, up to five: Target (2), say, with its own When for a different look in raids. A new row starts with its kind's plain look (no mark, plate color -) and its When on Always. A row for some units below its kind's row for more of them shows its Who and Distance in orange, as that row sets its parts first. Every row but Default has an x to take it off.

    Normal NPC - Enemy starts faded to 40% while an elite enemy is within about 28 yards of you (its When is the rule elite28, shown as text in its drawer and as a chip), so in a pull with an elite the elites stand out from the adds. Press its Edit to change it: elite11 or elite40 for 11 or 40 yards, or something like combat and elite28. Dead elites don't count: once the last one near you dies, the plates come back. An elite counts only while it has a plate, and the game drops plates for units out of view, so Off-screen plates in CineMode (under the grid, on to start with) turns on the game's Show Offscreen Nameplates in CineMode: an elite behind you keeps its plate, pinned to the screen's edge, and still counts. Unticked, it's off in CineMode. Either way your own setting comes back after.

    A row's When, in its drawer, works like a Standard Frames row: Always to start with, or only in the fights, targets and places you tick (or a rule from Edit), and only for the roles you tick. A row that doesn't fit right now leaves the plate to the next one. The Target Indicator row on the Nameplates page still says when marks show at all.

    StyleArrow, Chevron or Diamond (pointing down over the name), Glow (behind the health bar), Brackets or Carets (either side of the health bar), Corners (round it), Underline (under it), or -: no mark (and then nothing else on the Mark line). Aggro on you can also take your target's.
    ColorThe mark's: Gold; By reaction (red, yellow or green); Class colors (players in their class's color, everyone else by reaction); or Custom, from the swatch beside it. Aggro on you can also take your target's.
    Plate colorThe color for the parts ticked beside it: the unit's name and health bar, and on your target (and Aggro on you) the game's border round its health bar. - leaves it to the next row that fits the plate (the game's colors if none does); The game's keeps the game's, whatever the rows below say; Mark's is the mark's color; or Gold, By reaction, Class colors or Custom, as for the mark. Aggro on you can also take your target's: the parts the Target row ticks, in the Target row's color.
    OpacityOn the Plate line, the whole plate's opacity while the row fits it, mark or not; CineMode's own fade comes on top. On the Mark line, just the mark's. A plate's opacity and size move to a new row's over the Change time under the grid (1.5 seconds for a whole 100% to start with), turning back from where they are if the row changes again, so a quick look away from an elite and back barely shows.
    Size and MovingOn the Mark line, the mark's size; on the Plate line (retail only: WoW Forever's plates don't change size), the plate's, its mark too. Moving: whether the mark moves: the arrow, chevron and diamond bob, the brackets, carets and corners close in and out, the glow and underline pulse.
    All marksTwo heights for the marks drawn over the plate (arrow, chevron, diamond): Height with a plate, from just over the name, and Height without a plate, from where the plate's middle is while none of it shows. As the plate fades in or out, the marks glide between the two, the way the quest mark does.
    Outside CineMode

    What's left to the game

    Outside CineMode, Cinematic doesn't fade nameplates or names: the game's own nameplate and name options decide, as they would without the addon. A few things still apply:

    • Cinematic Plate Parts (class and role icons, the Target Indicator) show outside CineMode only on rows with Always ticked.
    • The Nameplate distance on the Graphics+ page sets how far away plates show, CineMode or not.
    • Beyond nameplate range, the game draws raid markers over units itself, as it does over your own head, and addons can't hide them.

    Visions add their own plates in CineMode: Quest Vision keeps up the plates of mobs you still need for a quest, with a golden !, and Duel Vision shows the two players dueling near you. The visions guide has more.

    Questions

    Good to know

    I hid friendly plates, but in a dungeon they're back.
    In dungeons and raids, friendly plates follow Friendly Plates in Dungeons and Raids instead of the grids: the game doesn't let addons fade them one by one there.
    I changed a setting mid-fight and nothing happened.
    The game only switches nameplates on or off out of combat. Your change applies as the fight ends.
    My target's plate shows even though its kind is unticked.
    That's the Target Nameplate grid, which starts with Always ticked for every kind. Untick a row there to let the main grids decide.
    Can I see plates farther away?
    Yes, with Nameplate distance on the Graphics+ page, up to the game's own limit.
