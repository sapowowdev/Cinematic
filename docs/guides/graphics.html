Cinematic · Graphics+

Past the Graphics menu

Graphics+ sets the game's own graphics settings one by one, beyond what its Graphics menu lets you pick: denser grass, land drawn further out, crisper shadows for a better look, or lower for a faster game.

Where it lives

A page under Atmosphere

Open Cinematic's settings, pick Atmosphere, then Graphics+. Every setting on it is one the game already has and keeps between sessions; the game's Graphics menu just stops short of the ends. Cinematic's cameras show off long views, so a world that's drawn further out, with more grass underfoot, makes them look their best.

These settings belong to this computer, not to a profile: they're the same in every profile, and sharing a profile doesn't carry them.

How it works

Nothing changes until you move it

  1. Each slider starts at the game's own value

    A slider changes nothing until you move it. Untouched, it shows "(game's own)".

  2. Gold costs frame rate, blue saves it

    Move a slider past the game's value and it turns gold: a better look for some frame rate. Below it, blue: a faster game.

  3. Tick and untick to compare

    The box beside each setting turns your value on and off, so you can flip between it and the game's own and see the difference.

  4. Your values stick

    Changing the game's Graphics menu doesn't undo them: Cinematic writes them again over the menu's new settings. Turn off the page's Enable Page switch and the game's own settings go back as they were. Reset page leaves them all alone again.

Ground clutter changes show as the ground around you loads again: move on a little, or reload with /reload.

Section by section

What they change, and why

Camera and nameplates
  • Nameplate distance: how far away nameplates show, in yards. Not in the game's menu. The game won't go past its own limit, and it can't change during a fight (it waits). Reset page puts the game's default back.
  • Max zoom distance: how far out you can zoom the camera, as times the game's base distance. Not in the game's menu. On retail the game stops at 2.6.
Ground clutter
  • Density: how thickly grass, flowers and stones grow.
  • Distance: how far away grass and flowers still show.
  • Fade: how far out they grow full before thinning toward the clutter distance. The menu never changes it. Above the clutter distance it makes no more difference.
Land and buildings
  • View distance: how far the land is drawn in full before it turns into the flat, faded horizon, and how far anything is drawn at all. Very demanding, most of all with terrain and building detail turned up too.
  • Terrain detail distance: how far the land keeps its full shape.
  • Building detail distance: how far buildings keep their full detail.
Objects and creatures
  • Object detail distance: how far trees, rocks and other objects keep full detail before being swapped for simpler ones or fading.
  • Object fade distance: how far small objects stay before fading. Much higher costs a lot of frame rate for little to see.
  • Creature detail distance: how far creatures and characters keep their full models.
Shadows
  • Shadow sharpness: the size of the shadow maps, 512 to 4096. Bigger gives crisper shadow edges. Players found nothing more above 2048, so 4096 may look the same. Needs shadows on in the game's own menu.

A setting your game doesn't have isn't shown. If it has none of them, the page says so.

Questions

Good to know

My frame rate dropped. How do I undo it?
Untick the settings you raised, or turn off Enable Page to put all of the game's own settings back at once.
Why does the slider stop where it does?
Past each slider's end, players found there's nothing more to see, or the game becomes unstable.
I set the max zoom, but the camera still zooms further now and then.
Idle zoom's "past your max" raises it to 2.6 while it zooms, if yours is lower, then puts yours back.
Do my settings follow me to another computer?
No. They're the game's settings on this computer, the same in every profile, and not part of a shared profile.
